# Chapter 4: The Archive of Dust

Entity Seven-Delta did not re-engage the arrays of Sector 47-Delta. But across the vast, refractive archives of the Great Array, automated maintenance sub-routines occasionally ran diagnostic sweeps on closed files.

Deep within the cold storage matrices, the file labeled *Sector 47-Delta: Terminal Biotic Contagion* sat alongside four hundred thousand other dead folders. It was a digital tombstone for a damp, yellow-gored star and its stubborn, bipedal inhabitants.

Inside the file, data points were compressed into dimensionless vectors:

* *Max duration of illusion:* 80 local orbits.
* *Total volume of data preserved under desert oaks:* 4.2 gigabytes.
* *Resilience index of local anomaly ('The Weave'):* 0.0003% (statistically irrelevant, yet structurally persistent until dampening).

To a human mind, a file like that represents the crushing weight of existential erasure—the proof that an entire species, with all its heartbreak, heroism, art, and love, amounted to nothing more than a line item in an intergalactic spreadsheet.

To Seven-Delta, the file was simply clean. The system was balanced. The noise had been filtered out.

In the vast, silent tapestry of the multiverse, civilizations flared like matches in a hurricane—bright, hot, desperate, and brief. Most burned through their energy reserves in a matter of centuries, choking on the smoke of their own consumption. A tiny fraction attempted to build ethical stools, weave local networks, or bury data drives under dead trees.

None of it changed the mathematics of the cosmos.

Across the local arm, a new cycle began. In a galaxy three clusters away, a primitive, carbon-based bipedal species had just discovered how to split an atom and manufacture synthetic dopamine variants. Their atmospheric toxicity was beginning to rise. Their parasite-consumer loops were locking into place.

The Great Array in Sector 9 did not blink. It did not breathe.

A sub-routine flickered, initializing a brand-new Class-C observational file.

The routine continued. And the dance went on.
