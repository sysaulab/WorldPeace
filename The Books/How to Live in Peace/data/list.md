
**Buddhist**

- **The Dalai Lama (Tenzin Gyatso)** — The obvious elder, but for good reason. He has spent decades listening to science, questioning Buddhist doctrine when it conflicts with evidence, and protecting the fragile Tibetan diaspora. He also loves a good philosophical tinker. If anyone would appreciate a cybernetic compass, it's him.

- **Thich Phap An** or another senior monastic in **Thich Nhat Hanh's Plum Village lineage** — Thầy himself passed in 2022, but his community continues his tradition of "engaged Buddhism": short-feedback mindfulness, radical interfaith openness, and hands-on protection of the vulnerable.

- **Kodo Nishimura** — A young Japanese monk and LGBTQ+ advocate. He embodies curiosity and respect in a tradition that needs both. He would bring a millennial voice.

**Christian (beyond the Pope)**

- **Father Greg Boyle, SJ** — Founder of Homeboy Industries in Los Angeles. He has spent decades in the gang rehabilitation space, practicing what he calls "kinship" — radical respect for the fragile, short feedback from lived experience, and zero interest in doctrinal contests.

- **Bishop William J. Barber II** — Leader of the Poor People's Campaign and a modern prophet in the tradition of MLK. His entire ministry is about systemic consequences: what does policy *do* to the poor and the vulnerable? He speaks the language of outcomes.

- **Reverend James Martin, SJ** — Jesuit priest and bridge-builder to LGBTQ+ Catholics. He has shown enormous courage in staying curious and protecting the fragile while remaining inside a hierarchical institution — exactly the tension the Cube is built to navigate.

- **Bishop Mariann Budde** (Episcopal Diocese of Washington) — Known for her quiet, direct moral clarity. She once publicly called out the President for using a church as a prop after clearing peaceful protesters. She lives the (+++) attractor with grace.

**Muslim**

- **Imam Feisal Abdul Rauf** — Founder of the Cordoba House and a longtime interfaith diplomat. He has spent his career translating between Islam and the West, asking not who is right, but what spiritual practices produce healing.

- **Dr. Omid Safi** — Scholar of Sufism at Duke, but more than an academic: he writes beautifully on the heart of Islam, on radical love, and on the cost of empire. He would speak the Cube's language fluently.

- **Sheikh Hamza Yusuf** (Zaytuna College) — Thoughtful, philosophically trained, and willing to critique both Muslim communities and Western materialism from a place of love rather than reactivity.

- **Mona Haydar** — A young Syrian-American poet and activist whose "Ask a Muslim" street booth and "Hijabi" rap video were acts of radical curiosity and feedback. She brings the raw, practical edge.

**Jewish (beyond Rabbi Beck)**

- **Rabbi David Rosen** — Former Chief Rabbi of Ireland, now international director of interreligious affairs for the AJC. Deeply involved in Vatican-Muslim-Jewish-Buddhist dialogue. He understands systemic consequences intimately.

- **Rabbi Sharon Brous (IKAR, Los Angeles)** — A leading voice in the Jewish renewal movement. She preaches a Judaism that is curious, anti-tribal, and fiercely protective of the marginalized. Her "Amen Effect" work on human connection fits the Cube precisely.

- **Rabbi Arthur Waskow** — Founder of the Shalom Center. Elder of Jewish environmental and justice activism. He thinks in systems, metaphors, and long arcs of history.

**Hindu / Dharmic**

- **Sadhvi Bhagawati Saraswati** (Parmarth Niketan, Rishikesh) — American-born, now a prominent Hindu spiritual teacher. Deeply engaged in interfaith work, environmental action, and women's empowerment. She embodies the "compassion without borders" that the Cube describes.

- **Sadhguru (Jaggi Vasudev)** — Controversial to some, but undeniably a systems thinker. His work on soil health (Save Soil movement) and inner ecology maps surprisingly well to the Cube's feedback-respect loops. He would engage the cybernetic framing enthusiastically.

**Indigenous / First Nations**

- **Elder Dave Courchene Jr. (Nii Gaani Aki Inini – Leading Earth Man)** — Anishinaabe elder, founder of the Turtle Lodge in Manitoba. He has spent his life protecting indigenous knowledge and inviting people of all cultures to sit in sacred circle. His teaching that "the land is the ultimate spiritual authority" resonates deeply with the Cube's definition of GOD as emergent complexity.

- **Chief Phil Lane Jr.** (Dakota/Chickasaw) — Creator of the Four Worlds International Institute, which works on indigenous-led systems change. He literally speaks the language of systems and healing attractors. A natural ally.

**Wild Cards (non-traditional but deeply relevant)**

- **Bayo Akomolafe** — A Nigerian philosopher-poet rooted in Yoruba wisdom and post-humanist thought. He speaks of "making sanctuary" and "slowing down in the ruins." His thinking is uncannily aligned with the Cube — he would add a decolonial and poetic layer.

- **Dr. Iain McGilchrist** — A psychiatrist and philosopher whose work on brain hemispheres (*The Master and His Emissary*) is essentially a cybernetic diagnosis of civilizational drift toward the (---) attractor. Not a spiritual leader per se, but a thinker whose work bridges neuroscience and mysticism. He would be a fascinating interlocutor.
