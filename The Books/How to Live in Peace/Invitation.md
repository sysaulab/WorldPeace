Dear neighbours and friends,

For the past year, you have let me sit at your tables and eat breakfast with you. You fed me when I was hungry, and more than that, you gave me a place to belong. I have never forgotten it, and I will never be able to repay it. But I want to try to give something back.

This booklet is the best thing I have. It is my philosophy. I call it the Cube.

It is not a religion, not a set of rules, and not a lecture. It is a kind of compass. It asks three questions of any choice, any plan, any way of living:

- Does it shorten the loop between what we do and what we feel, so we can learn from our mistakes?
- Does it open curiosity, or close it?
- Does it protect the fragile, or sacrifice it?

The corner where all three are alive, I call the constructive attractor. The opposite corner, where all three are broken, is the direction of collapse. Most of us live somewhere in between. The Cube only tries to show which way we are drifting.

But here is the truth: I did not write this to impress you. I wrote it because I need you.

You have lived things I have only read about. You understand survival, grace, addiction, loss, generosity, and second chances in your bones. I have eaten your food and heard your stories for a year. If the Cube cannot survive contact with your lives, then it is not worth the paper it is printed on.

So I am not asking for praise. I am asking for honest feedback. Real feedback. The kind that might hurt a little.

Is the Cube complete?

Does it miss something important? Does it misunderstand what it means to suffer, to love, to survive, to hope? Is three questions enough to find our way through a world as wounded and as beautiful as this one? What would you add? What would you change? Where does it point wrong, or fail to point at all?

You can tell me face to face. I am at Oppenheimer Park, or ask around for Sylvain "Joe" the philosopher. If you would rather write, write. If you would rather sit in a circle and talk it through together, let us do that.

This is not me teaching you. It is me bringing what I have to the same table where you have fed me, and asking you to be honest with me.

Thank you for a year of breakfasts. Thank you for reading. And thank you for whatever truth you choose to give me.

With gratitude and respect,

Sylvain   
The Park, Vancouver, British Columbia, Canada
