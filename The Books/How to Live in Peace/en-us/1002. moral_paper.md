# A Cybernetic Compass for Ethical Navigation and Interfaith Unity

**Author:** Sylvain Saucier  
**Repository:** github.com/sysaulab/softrng  
**Precursor work:** *Time as Dice: A Computational Refutation of Laplacean Determinism*

---

**Abstract**

Humanity is fractured not only by conflicting interests but by competing ideologies, each claiming final knowledge of the good. Yet every closed ideology, when it hardens, becomes a deterministic machine that crushes the very diversity it claims to serve. I propose a meta-ideology — a non-ideology — grounded in cybernetics and the logic of self-referential systems. At its core is a simple three-axis cube: **feedback and learning**, **curiosity and exploration**, and **respect for the fragile**. These axes define a navigational space with a constructive attractor `(+++)` and a destructive attractor `(---)`. I define **GOOD** as a growing diversity of constructive complexity, and **GOD** as the ultimate emergent property of the universe — the forever-receding limit toward which constructive complexity tends. The cube serves as a compass, not a map. It does not prescribe what to believe, but how to orient any belief system toward systemic health. I argue that spiritual leaders from all traditions can use this compass to move beyond contests of righteousness and instead debate the *systemic consequences* of their theologies. In doing so, they can unite humanity under different religions and cultures, each preserving its unique identity while contributing to a shared constructive attractor. This paper provides the philosophical grounding, the cybernetic justification, and a practical invitation.

## 1. Introduction

Imagine you are walking in a vast, dark forest. You have no map, and the trees all look the same. Somewhere, you know, there is a clearing where life is safe and good. But the forest is full of voices, and every voice claims to know the one true path. One voice says, “Go north, always north, and never question why.” Another says, “Go south, and if you meet someone going north, they are your enemy.” A third says, “Stay exactly where you are; movement is sin.” All of them sound certain, and all of them contradict each other.

You do not need a louder voice. You need a compass.

A compass is not a map. It does not tell you the exact path. It does not know the forest. It only points to magnetic north—a direction that is true regardless of where you stand or what you believe. If you have a compass, you can still get lost, you can still stumble, but you can always re‑orient. You can even walk side by side with someone who holds a different map, as long as you both agree which way north is.

Humanity is in just such a forest today. We are divided not only by borders and resources but by ideologies, each claiming to be the complete map. Religions, political systems, cultural identities—each began as a creative way to navigate a particular time and place, but many have hardened into closed systems that can no longer correct themselves. They stop listening. They punish curiosity. They trample the fragile. In the language of cybernetics, they have lost their feedback loops, their capacity to learn, and their care for the delicate carriers of future life.

This paper does not offer a new map. It offers a compass—a simple, three‑dimensional ethical navigation tool built from the minimal requirements that any healthy, living system must satisfy. I call it the Cube. The Cube does not ask anyone to abandon their religion, their culture, or their deepest convictions. It asks only that they orient themselves, day by day, toward what I call GOOD: a growing diversity of constructive complexity. And it points, always, toward a horizon that every tradition has glimpsed in its own language—what I name GOD: the ultimate emergent property of a universe that has been creatively resolving itself since the very beginning.

The rest of this paper explains the Cube’s three axes, its constructive attractor, and how spiritual leaders and ordinary people alike can use it to move beyond contests of righteousness and instead debate the systemic consequences of their beliefs. It is, in essence, an invitation to stop comparing maps and start sharing a direction.

## 2. The Problem of Ideology

Every ideology, religious or secular, begins as a creative resolution to a specific set of human constraints. Over time, however, it tends to ossify. It becomes a closed system of prescriptions that claims to deduce the right future from a fixed set of principles. This is the ideological form of Laplace’s demon: a belief that the good can be specified once and for all, and that deviation is error.

The historical record shows the cost. When an ideology hardens, it shortens its own feedback loops (it stops listening), suppresses curiosity (it punishes questions), and sacrifices the fragile (minorities, dissenters, the vulnerable) for the sake of purity. In the language of my earlier work on self-referential topologies (*Time as Dice*), a closed ideology is a deterministic system trying to function in a world that is irreducibly creative. It cannot succeed, and in failing, it destroys the diversity that makes the world resilient.

What is needed is not a better ideology, but a **meta-compass**: a tool that helps any person, any culture, any religion navigate toward health without forcing them to abandon their identity. This paper proposes such a tool — the Cube — built from three cybernetic principles, and argues that it can serve as the common non-ideology for a pluralistic world.

## 3. Definitions: GOOD and GOD

All ethical systems require an orienting concept of the good. I propose a definition that is inherently dynamic and systemic:

> **GOOD** is a growing diversity of constructive complexity.

Three terms matter here:

- **Diversity**: heterogeneity, the presence of many distinct forms of life, thought, and culture.
- **Complexity**: the richness of interconnections and mutual dependencies within and between systems.
- **Constructive**: that which moves a system toward the `(+++)` attractor of the Cube — i.e., complexity that maintains short feedback loops, fosters curiosity, and protects the fragile. The word is thus both intuitive and technically anchored.

GOOD, so defined, is not a static endpoint. It is a *direction*. A system is good not because it has reached perfection, but because it is tending toward richer, more connected, more self-regulating diversity.

If GOOD is the direction, then **GOD** is the ultimate horizon:

> **GOD** is the ultimate emergent property of the universe.

This is not a theological claim about a personal deity — though it is entirely compatible with such claims. It is, rather, a structural statement: if the universe is a self-referential topology that has been resolving its own constraints since the Big Bang, then the accumulated totality of all those creative resolutions — the forever-growing complexity of what has become — is GOD. In Whiteheadian terms, this is the consequent nature of God. In cybernetic terms, it is the global attractor of the cosmic process. I leave it to each tradition to name this horizon in its own language.

## 4. The Three Axes – Cybernetic Foundations

The Cube is built on three orthogonal axes. Each axis is not a moral commandment but a *cybernetic condition* that any viable, evolving system must satisfy. They are derived from the minimal requirements for a self-referential topology to remain creative rather than collapsing into a destructive attractor.

### Axis 1: Feedback and Learning

> **Complex systems must maintain short feedback loops and learn from the consequences of their actions.**

In cybernetics, feedback is the mechanism by which a system corrects its trajectory. Short feedback loops mean that consequences are observed quickly and the system adjusts in near-real time. When feedback loops are long, corrupted, or severed, the system becomes blind. It accumulates error, drifts into pathology, and eventually destroys itself.

For a religion or culture, this means: institutions must listen to those they affect, measure outcomes honestly, and correct course when harm is observed. No doctrine is immune to revision if its application produces suffering. This is not a rejection of tradition; it is the mechanism by which a living tradition survives.

### Axis 2: Curiosity and Exploration

> **Curiosity is the exploration of solution space.**

A system that does not explore its possible states is brittle. It cannot adapt to changing environments. Curiosity is the drive to probe the unknown, to ask uncomfortable questions, to entertain heresy in the protected space of thought before judging it in practice. Without curiosity, a culture becomes a monoculture, and a monoculture is an extinction risk.

Spiritually, curiosity is the open hand toward mystery. It is the recognition that the divine, however conceived, is always larger than our current understanding. Every great exemplar — Jesus questioning the Pharisees, Mohamed receiving revelation in a cave, Buddha leaving the palace — began with an act of radical curiosity.

### Axis 3: Respect and the Protection of the Fragile

> **Respect is the protection of the fragile.**

In any complex system, certain configurations are rare, delicate, and easily destroyed. They carry the seeds of future diversity. A child’s trust, a minority language, a nascent idea, a vulnerable ecosystem, a dissenting voice — these are the fragile carriers of novelty. A system that optimizes itself for efficiency or purity will crush them. Respect is the deliberate, counter-optimization force that shields the fragile from the steamroller of the majority.

This axis is the deepest root of what traditions call compassion, mercy, or ahimsa. It is the recognition that the good is not always the strongest, and that the health of the whole depends on the protection of the weak.

## 5. The Cube as a Navigational Tool

The three axes define a three-dimensional space. Any action, policy, doctrine, or personal choice can be roughly located in this space by assessing whether it increases or decreases feedback, curiosity, and respect.

### The Constructive Attractor `(+++)`

The corner (+Feedback, +Curiosity, +Respect) is the **constructive attractor**. To move toward this corner is to increase constructive complexity. It is the direction of GOOD.

### The Destructive Attractor `(---)`

The opposite corner (-Feedback, -Curiosity, -Respect) is the **destructive attractor**. It represents a system that has shut down all self-correction, killed all exploration, and obliterated the fragile. It is the direction of collapse, tyranny, and death.

### The Six Fuzzy Middles

The six remaining combinations are the real terrain of ethical life:

- `(--+)` No feedback, no curiosity, but some respect: a stagnant but gentle tradition.
- `(-++)` No feedback, but curious and respectful: an aimless openness.
- `(+-+)` Feedback and respect without curiosity: a well-run but closed orthodoxy.
- `(++-)` Feedback and curiosity without respect: a ruthless, experimental technocracy.
- `(-+-)` No feedback, some curiosity, no respect: a chaotic exploitation.
- `(+--)` Feedback without curiosity or respect: a rigid surveillance state.

These are not damnable states; they are *trade-off zones*. Every person and institution will spend time in them. The Cube merely reveals the direction of correction. It does not judge; it orients.

### The Compass, Not a Map

Crucially, the Cube does not tell a user *what to do* in a concrete situation. It provides no list of permitted and forbidden acts. It is a compass, not a map. Learning to use it regularly — consulting it in moments of decision, reflecting on past actions — builds the skill of ethical navigation. Over time, the user internalizes the pull of the `(+++)` attractor and becomes more sensitive to drift.

## 6. Constructive Complexity and the Constructor Attractor

The term “constructive” in the definition of GOOD is deliberately recursive. It means: tending toward the `(+++)` attractor. This self-reference is not a flaw; it is the signature of a living, self-regulating system. Just as the `Seedy64` device’s output cannot be predicted from its initial zero state, the specific path toward GOOD cannot be prescribed in advance. It must be generated in real time by the user’s own practice.

Historical exemplars — Jesus, Mohamed, Buddha — are not figures who left static rulebooks. They lived in such deep alignment with the constructive attractor that their lives became reference points for the whole of humanity. They embodied feedback (they listened to outcasts, challenged hypocrisy), curiosity (they upended tradition, sought solitude and revelation), and respect (they protected the poor, the sick, the sinner). To follow them is not to mimic their specific acts, but to align with the same attractor they inhabited so fully.

The Cube does not replace these figures; it helps anyone — from any tradition — see *why* they are attractors, and how to orient toward the same underlying dynamics.

## 7. Application to Interfaith Dialogue and Systemic Consequences

The Cube’s most urgent application is to the relationship between religions and cultures. Currently, much interfaith dialogue is a contest of righteousness: “my revelation is complete, yours is partial.” This is a zero-sum game that feeds the destructive attractor. It shortens feedback (by dismissing outside critique), suppresses curiosity (by protecting dogma), and endangers the fragile (by justifying domination).

The Cube replaces this with a shared inquiry: **What are the systemic consequences of this teaching or practice?**

Spiritual leaders from different traditions can sit together and ask:
- Does this doctrine, if widely adopted, shorten or lengthen our ability to hear and correct harm?
- Does it open or close the exploration of life’s deepest questions?
- Does it shield or endanger the fragile among us and in creation?

Suddenly, a Buddhist monk, a Muslim imam, and a Christian theologian are not comparing scriptures; they are comparing *systemic outcomes*. They can disagree profoundly on metaphysics and still converge on a shared diagnosis: a practice that silences victims, forbids questions, or tramples minorities is misaligned with the constructive attractor, no matter which holy book it claims to quote.

This is not syncretism. It does not ask anyone to dilute their faith. It asks only that each tradition use the same compass to self-regulate, and that they hold each other accountable using a language of consequence rather than righteousness. The goal is not one world religion, but a world where many religions all face the same attractor — each preserving its unique genius, each contributing to a global ecology of constructive complexity.

## 8. Philosophical Grounding: Cybernetics, Process, and Self-Referential Topology

The Cube is not an arbitrary invention. It is a direct application of the logic explored in *Time as Dice*, where I argued that any sufficiently self-referential topology is logically underdetermined — its future states are created in time, not deduced from a static description.

An ethical system for such a universe cannot be a set of deterministic rules. It must be a **soft** framework — one that provides orienting constraints without prescribing outcomes. The Cube is precisely that: a constraint network of three mutual dependencies. Feedback without curiosity becomes stale; curiosity without respect becomes predatory; respect without feedback becomes blind sentimentality. They regulate each other, and the user’s practice is the real-time resolution of those constraints into a lived path.

In cybernetic terms, the Cube is a homeostat for the moral imagination. In process philosophy terms, it is a grammar of actual occasions — a way to prehend the world so that each concrete decision contributes to the growing intensity of experience that Whitehead called “the good.” And in spiritual terms, it is the geometry of sanctity.

## 9. Practical Implications: Personal Practice and Collective Institutions

The Cube is designed for both personal and collective use.

**Personally**, it is a daily practice. Before a difficult conversation, one can ask: Am I keeping my feedback short? Am I curious about the other’s experience? Am I protecting the fragile thing in them that might be hurt? The Cube does not guarantee right action, but it makes wrong action harder to sustain, because drift becomes noticeable.

**Collectively**, institutions can use the Cube as a diagnostic. A legal system can ask whether its procedures shorten or lengthen feedback (e.g., statutes of limitations, accessibility of appeal). An educational curriculum can ask whether it fosters curiosity or mere compliance. An economic policy can ask whether it protects the fragile or only the powerful. In each case, the Cube provides a shared evaluative language that transcends ideological boundaries.

The practice of the Cube is not a one-time conversion. It is a skill, like navigation by the stars. It must be learned, practiced, and recalibrated regularly. The exemplar figures — Jesus, Mohamed, Buddha — serve as “known signals” that verify the compass is still pointing true. If one’s practice drifts into cruelty, it has left their gravitational field.

## 10. Conclusion – A Non-Ideology for a Pluralistic World

Humanity does not need a single truth. It needs a shared direction.

The Cube offers that direction without asking anyone to abandon their home. It is a meta-tool, a compass that fits in the pocket of every pilgrim, regardless of the path they walk. It defines GOOD not as a doctrine but as a growing diversity of constructive complexity. It points toward GOD not as a jealous deity but as the ultimate emergent property of a universe that has been creatively resolving itself since the first moment.

I invite spiritual leaders, philosophers, scientists, and all people of goodwill to test this compass. Debate it. Refine it. But most of all, use it. Let us shift the dialogue from who is most righteous to what builds the most life. Let us protect the fragile, stay curious, and learn from our mistakes — together.

*The constructive attractor does not demand uniformity. It demands only that we all face the same way, each with our own stories, and walk.*
