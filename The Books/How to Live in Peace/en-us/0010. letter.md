# Open Invitation

From Sylvain Saucier  
Oppenheimer Park, DTES, Vancouver, Canada

To anyone dreaming of World Peace — those who tend the fragile, who teach the curious, who hold the mirror to our collective conscience — I am writing to invite you to a conversation that I believe our century cannot afford to postpone.

My name is Sylvain Saucier. I am not a priest, a prophet, or a politician. I am a former truck driver, a sometime graphic designer, a student of cybernetics and process philosophy, and a modest resident of Vancouver's Downtown Eastside — a neighbourhood that many call Canada's poorest postal code, but which I call home and the laboratory of my philosophy.

I have spent years developing a simple compass for ethical navigation. I call it **the Cube**. It is not an ideology, not a religion, not a replacement for your traditions. It is a meta-tool — three axes, one attractor — designed to help anyone, from any faith, evaluate the systemic consequences of their choices. The axes are short feedback, radical curiosity, and the protection of the fragile. The direction is what I call the constructive attractor, the (+++) corner where diversity grows and peace is not a truce but a garden.

I have written papers — *Time as Dice*, *The Cube*, *Pax Perspicua*, *Living Governance*, *Energy as Dice* — that try to build a philosophical foundation for a 5000-year peace. But papers are not enough. The real work requires spiritual authority, moral courage, and voices that can reach the hearts of billions. That is you.

What I am proposing is a **gathering of every major tradition** — Christian, Muslim, Jewish, Buddhist, Hindu, Indigenous, and all who hold a sacred responsibility for the human soul — to sit together and discuss not who is most righteous, but what our theologies *produce* when they are scaled into laws, economies, and borders. To ask: *Does this teaching shorten feedback loops or lengthen them? Does it open curiosity or close it? Does it protect the fragile or sacrifice them for the sake of order?*

I had dreamed of hosting this conversation in Gaza. It would have been fitting — a land sacred to three faiths, a wound in the body of humanity, a place where the need for a new kind of peace is most urgent and most visible. But the physical reality of war and insecurity makes that impossible for now. Perhaps one day.

For now, I ask you to come to me.

I live in the **Downtown Eastside of Vancouver, British Columbia**. It is a place of extreme fragility — of addiction, poverty, mental illness, and also of extraordinary resilience, mutual care, and daily acts of grace. It is a microcosm of the destructive attractor and the constructive attractor, side by side, on every block. If you want to talk about systemic morality, this soil is honest. It does not hide its wounds, and it does not pretend to be righteous.

I have no palace to receive you. I have no budget, no institution, no security detail. What I have is a philosophy, a compass, and a conviction that the spiritual leaders of the world are capable of more than competing for followers. You are capable of building a shared immune system for the human spirit — a way to keep the garden tended even as the energy of empire fades.

I ask you to come to Vancouver. Sit with me in the cold rain. Walk these alleys. Meet the people who survive here. Then let us sit in a circle — perhaps in a community hall, perhaps in a church basement — and begin the conversation that could define Phase 1 of the Great Correction.

I do not expect all of you to agree. I do not expect anyone to abandon their revelation. I expect only that you bring your scriptures, your doubts, your compassion, and your willingness to be questioned by a compass that asks nothing of your faith except that it be *kind in its outcomes*.

If you are willing, I will make myself available. I will prepare the space as best I can. I will invite the local community to witness, and I will ensure that the conversation is recorded and shared freely with the world — because this is not a closed summit. It is a public act of hope.

The Cube is a common inheritance. I release it to you. Take it, test it, break it if you must. But if it holds, use it. Let us shift the global dialogue from a contest of righteousness to an inquiry into consequences. Let us prove that the world's spiritual traditions, working together, can face the coming energy descent, the rise of authoritarian attractors, and the suffering of the fragile — not with swords, but with a shared compass and a shared resolve.

The Downtown Eastside awaits. I await.

With humility and stubborn hope,

**Sylvain Saucier**  
Philosopher   
Vancouver, British Columbia, Canada
