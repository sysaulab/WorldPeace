
## Chapter: The Cube Diagnostic – A Spiritual Health Check

The Cube does not judge. It merely reveals direction. But direction is often invisible from the inside of a tradition; we mistake our own momentum for righteousness. The following diagnostic is offered as a mirror, not a microscope. It is designed for internal use—by you, the living members of a tradition—and it assumes good faith. No one who uses it honestly will come out smelling of perfection. The point is to notice the drift and gently correct it.

### The Three Vital Signs Self-Examination

Below are questions for each axis. There is no final scoreboard, but as you answer, a pattern will emerge. If most of your answers cluster around the negative, you are drifting toward the destructive attractor; if they cluster around the positive, you are facing the constructive attractor. The middle is where nearly all of us live—it’s the school, not the final exam.

#### Axis 1: Feedback & Learning

*The ability to hear the consequences of your actions and adjust.*

| Question (for your community / institution) | Often (–) | Sometimes (0) | Rarely (+) |
|--------------------------------------------|-----------|---------------|------------|
| When someone outside our tradition criticises us, do we seriously consider the critique, or do we dismiss it as ignorance or malice? | Dismiss | Sometimes consider | Welcome it as a mirror |
| Do we have regular, safe channels for internal dissent—people who can say “this policy is hurting people” without fear of reprisal? | No channels | Some, but risky | Yes, built into our structure |
| When a decision or teaching causes real harm (measured by the pain of those affected), do we revisit that decision or double down? | Double down | Hesitate, but may change under pressure | Proactively correct |
| Do the most powerless people in our orbit—the janitor, the child, the newcomer—have an easy way to speak and be heard by those in authority? | No easy way | Occasionally | Deliberately invited |
| How quickly do we find out when something is going wrong? Months? Weeks? Days? Immediately? | Months+ | Weeks | Days or instantly |

**Red flag:** If you find yourself saying “They just don’t understand our truth” whenever outsiders point out harm, your feedback loops are dangerously long.

#### Axis 2: Curiosity & Exploration

*The drive to understand what you do not yet understand—including about yourself.*

| Question | Often (–) | Sometimes (0) | Rarely (+) |
|----------|-----------|---------------|------------|
| Do we have a list of “forbidden questions”—subjects that are off the table, ideas that must never be entertained even hypothetically? | Yes, many | A few | No forbidden questions, only questions we haven’t yet explored |
| When a young person or a new member asks “why do we believe this?”, is the typical first response to educate, to defend, or to reprimand? | Reprimand / shut down | Defend | Educate and explore together |
| Do we study other traditions, other philosophies, or the sciences with genuine openness, or only to refute them? | Refutation only | Selective | Genuine engagement |
| Is doubt treated as a weakness, a sin, a temporary phase to be cured—or as a sacred part of the journey? | Sin / weakness | Phase to be cured | Sacred, necessary |
| Does the highest authority in our tradition ever publicly admit they don’t know something, or do they project omniscience? | Never admit ignorance | Occasionally, reluctantly | Regularly and with humility |

**Red flag:** If your community’s language is saturated with absolute certainties and those who express uncertainty are marginalized or shamed, curiosity is dying.

---

#### Axis 3: Respect & Protection of the Fragile

*The deliberate care for what is easily broken—people, ecosystems, hopes.*

| Question | Often (–) | Sometimes (0) | Rarely (+) |
|----------|-----------|---------------|------------|
| Who are the fragile in our orbit? (List them.) Now ask: do our budgets, our schedules, our teaching priorities reflect a priority on protecting them, or do they mainly serve the already-secure? | Serve the secure | Mixed | Prioritize the fragile |
| When a member of our community falls—through addiction, poverty, divorce, mental illness—is the first instinct to help or to hide the shame? | Hide / distance | Help privately | Wrap around publicly |
| Does our teaching ever justify the subordination, exclusion, or punishment of a group of people because of their identity, their past, or their beliefs? | Yes, explicitly | Implicitly | Never; we challenge such teachings |
| Do we speak about the natural world as a resource to be used, or as a fragile partner to be tended? | Resource | Neutral | Fragile partner |
| When we are in conflict with another group, do we deliberately seek to protect civilians, children, or the helpless on the other side—or does our rhetoric dehumanize them? | Dehumanize | Ambivalent | Protect actively, even at cost |

**Red flag:** If your community’s talk about “love” and “compassion” is never matched by institutional sacrifice of power or comfort in favor of the weakest, respect is hollow.

### Interpreting the Constellation

Sit with your answers—preferably with a small group of trusted, honest members. You don’t need to add up numbers. Instead, hold the whole picture and ask:

- *If we were a patient lying in a hospital bed, which vital sign is fluttering?*
- *Are we drifting from the (+++) corner toward one of the fuzzy middles—say, a rigid but gentle traditionalism, or a curious but reckless experiment without protection for the weak?*
- *What single, concrete change could we make in the next month to shorten a feedback loop, open a door of curiosity, or shelter one fragile population more thoroughly?*

The change doesn’t have to be grand. It could be as simple as starting a meeting where the elders listen in silence to the youth; or inviting a guest speaker from a tradition you normally oppose, just to hear their story; or diverting 5% of the budget to a fund controlled by the most marginalized members.

The Cube doesn’t demand that you leave your theology. It only demands that you *orient*—and the diagnostic is the act of turning the map so you can see which way you’re walking.

### Spotting the Destructive Drift Early

History gives us a short checklist of early-warning symptoms. Any one of these, on its own, might be a passing cold. All three together are a fever that has burned down whole civilizations:

1. **Scapegoating the fragile for internal anxiety.** The community begins to blame its problems on a vulnerable minority—the heretic, the immigrant, the modernist, the traditionalist. Their pain becomes invisible or righteous.
2. **The rise of a “pure” language.** Words like “authentic,” “uncorrupted,” “original,” “real” start being used as weapons. Any deviation is labeled betrayal or contamination.
3. **Secrecy and the punishment of truth-tellers.** Whistleblowers are silenced, abused, or exiled. Internal dissidents are cast as agents of the enemy.

If you see these signs, the diagnostic is not a gentle mirror anymore—it is a fire alarm. Pull it.

### A Simple Rite for Collective Diagnosis

You could adapt the following for your own context:

1. **Gather** a representative cross-section of the community—elders, youth, those on the margins, those in authority.
2. **Light a candle or sit in silence** to acknowledge that this is a sacred act of self-examination, not a bureaucratic meeting.
3. **Read aloud** the three axes and their questions. Let each person mark their own private answers.
4. **Go around the circle** and, without debate, let each person name one thing they are proud of (a sign of the constructive attractor) and one thing that worries them (a sign of drift).
5. **Together**, choose one concrete action to strengthen a vital sign, and name who will be responsible for implementing it.
6. **Set a date** to return and see if the needle has moved.

The rite itself is a feedback loop. Over time, the community builds the muscle of self-regulation, turning the Cube from a concept into a living practice.
