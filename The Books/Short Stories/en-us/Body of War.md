
**The Day the Legs and the Arms Stopped Speaking**

There was once a boy named Leo who had very strong legs and very clever arms. His legs could run faster than any dog, leap over streams, and kick a ball clean over the neighbour's shed. His arms could throw stones with perfect aim, build towers of driftwood, and hug his grandmother so tightly she'd squeak.

One morning, Leo woke up to find his legs and his arms in a terrible argument.

"My legs are the most important," said the right leg, stomping the floor. "Without us, Leo would just be a lump on the carpet. He couldn't go anywhere. He couldn't chase the ice-cream cart. He couldn't even stand up to wave."

"Ridiculous," said the right arm, crossing itself indignantly. "Without us, Leo couldn't eat that ice-cream. He couldn't catch a ball. He couldn't draw a picture or row a boat. We are the ones who *do* things. You legs just carry us around like a clumsy cart."

The left leg joined in. "A cart? Without us, you arms would just dangle in the air like useless noodles!"

The left arm shot back, "At least we don't trip over pebbles and scrape our knees!"

And so it went. The legs accused the arms of being lazy. The arms accused the legs of being thick and unthinking. The toes wiggled in protest. The fingers curled into fists. Soon, the whole body was a shouting match of elbows and knees and grumpy tendons.

Finally, the right leg bellowed: "Let's settle this. Leo must choose. Which side is better? Which side is *him*? He can only be a leg-person or an arm-person. Pick one!"

The left arm nodded stiffly. "Yes. Choose. Us or them."

Leo, who was only a boy and had never been asked such a silly question, opened his mouth to answer. But something stopped him. He looked at his legs—strong, faithful, dusted with grass stains. He looked at his arms—sinewy, clever, freckled from the sun. They were both *his*. They had always been his. How could he choose one without losing a piece of himself?

"Please," said Leo. "Why do I have to choose to begin with?"

The legs scoffed. "Because we are in conflict! Conflict demands a winner!"

The arms snorted. "Because we cannot both be right! One must be the champion!"

Leo sat down—or rather, he tried to. But his legs, offended, refused to bend. And his arms, equally offended, refused to brace his fall. He toppled sideways like a felled tree, cracking his elbow on the floorboard and twisting his ankle in the process.

"Ow," said Leo quietly. "That hurt."

Now, a small voice emerged from somewhere deep in his chest—not his heart, not his lungs, but the quiet, ancient place where knowing lives. It said:

*Legs, can you lift a spoon to his mouth?*
The legs grumbled. No, they could not.

*Arms, can you walk him to the stream for a drink?*
The arms flushed. No, they could not.

*Legs, who carries the arms to the high shelf?*
The legs fell silent.

*Arms, who steadies the legs when they stumble on a root?*
The arms looked at the floor.

And the quiet voice continued: *You are not two kingdoms. You are not rivals. You are not even neighbours. You are the same creature. When you fight, Leo falls. When you cooperate, Leo climbs trees, catches fireflies, and dances at festivals. There is no champion here. There is only a boy who needs his whole self.*

The legs looked at the arms. The arms looked at the legs. They had been so busy demanding to be *chosen* that they had forgotten the only thing that mattered: they were already *chosen*—chosen by Leo, chosen by the same heart, chosen by the same blood that rushed through them both.

Slowly, tentatively, the right leg bent. The left arm reached down. Together, they helped Leo stand. Then the arms reached for a fallen apple on the kitchen counter, while the legs carried him toward the door. Leo bit into the apple, walked into the sunshine, and felt, for the first time all morning, completely whole.

He didn't choose. That was the point.
