This is a fantastic concept. A comedic, micro-historical epic. Here is a detailed plan for your novel.

**Novel Title:** *The Wanting of Lake Meridian*

**Logline:** In a single, modest lake and its surrounding shores, the entire tumultuous, absurd, and petty history of the modern world from 1925 to 2025 is played out by a cast of hapless nations, all vying for the best fishing spots, the most picturesque sunsets, and the coveted title of "Most Reasonable Superpower."

---

### The World: Lake Meridian and its Inhabitants

**The Setting:** Lake Meridian is a large, self-contained body of water. It is the alpha and omega of this world. The "continent" is its shoreline, the "oceans" are the deeper, murkier parts of the lake, and the "islands" are small, rocky outcrops.

**The Major Powers (The "Nations"):**

- **The Grand Duchy of Upper Shoria (USA):** Located on the largest, sunniest, and most resource-rich northern shore. They have the biggest, most ostentatious cottages, the most powerful motorboats, and a deep-seated belief that they are the only ones who truly understand how to run the lake. Their catchphrase: "We saved this lake once, and we can do it again."
- **The Collective of Lower Shoria (USSR/Russia):** Occupies the vast, cold, and windy eastern shore. They have a lot of land, a lot of timber, and a perpetually grumpy attitude. They believe Upper Shoria is loud and vulgar. They are prone to "restructuring" their entire shoreline every few decades.
- **The Federation of Brindlemark (Western Europe):** A collection of small, elegant, and often bickering plots on the southern shore. They have the best wine, the oldest history, and a tendency to form overly complex committees to solve problems like "whose turn is it to bring the potato salad."
- **The Kingdom of Albion's Reach (UK):** A prominent, slightly damp peninsula on the western shore, connected to the mainland by a very narrow, historically significant isthmus. They are obsessed with their "special relationship" with Upper Shoria and their glorious, but rapidly receding, naval history (they used to have the best rowboats).
- **The Coastal Provinces (China):** A massive, ancient, and industrious district on the far, misty western shore. For a long time, they were ignored by the other shores, but they have recently started building enormous, efficient, and slightly intimidating docks at a pace that has everyone else nervous.
- **The Southern Archipelago (Oceania):** A chain of small, friendly, but frequently overlooked islands. They are known for their excellent grilled fish and their tendency to get dragged into Upper Shoria's military exercises.

**The Timeline of Lake Meridian (1925-2025):**

**Act I: The Roaring Twenties & The Great Depression (1925-1939)**
- **1925:** The "Roaring Twenties" on the lake. Upper Shoria is booming, selling "miracle" fishing lures to everyone. The parties at their cottages are legendary. Brindlemark is still recovering from the "Great War of the Flowerbeds" (1914-1918) and is trying to enjoy a quiet cup of tea.
- **1929:** The Great Crash. The price of premium fishing lures plummets. Upper Shoria's economy tanks. A global sense of malaise sets in. Everyone starts blaming everyone else for the bad weather.

**Act II: The Great Lakes War (1939-1945)**
- The "Biggest Argument." A dispute over a disputed patch of reeds escalates. The figure of **Adolf Schicklgruber** (no relation) rises to power in the small, disgruntled nation of Germania on the southern shore. He starts making aggressive speeches about needing "living space" for his sun loungers.
- The war is fought with water balloons, sabotaged picnic baskets, and aggressive propaganda leaflets. The "Holocaust" is the systematic draining of a small, culturally vibrant pond that Germania found "aesthetically displeasing."
- It ends with Upper Shoria, Albion's Reach, and the Collective of Lower Shoria forming an unlikely alliance to dunk Germania's leader in the lake. The "Atomic Splash" ends the conflict, creating a large, glistening ripple that everyone agrees never to speak of again.

**Act III: The Cold War (1945-1991)**
- A "Staring Contest" across the lake. Upper Shoria and Lower Shoria divide the lake into spheres of influence. The "Iron Curtain" is a particularly ugly and poorly-maintained floating fence.
- The "Space Race" is a competition to build the first and most impressive floating gazebo. The "Cuban Missile Crisis" is the "Bay of Pigsy's Pier Incident," where Lower Shoria tries to install a very loud, very annoying wind chime on a small island, and Upper Shoria blockades the entire lake in response. The world holds its breath for 13 days.
- Proxy wars are fought over which shore has the best recipe for potato salad.

**Act IV: The New World Order (1991-2008)**
- Lower Shoria's floating fence collapses, and they spend the next decade trying to sell off their old, rusted sections for scrap.
- Upper Shoria becomes the "World's Only Remaining Superpower." They are insufferable. They try to organize "fun days" for the whole lake, but no one else is having fun.
- **The "War on Terror"** is the "War on Litter." A particularly aggressive goose nips a Shorian diplomat. Upper Shoria invades the "suspicious" looking reed beds of the Coastal Provinces, looking for "weapons of mass destruction" (which turn out to be a few surprisingly large snapping turtles).

**Act V: The Modern Era & The Climate Crisis (2008-2025)**
- **2008:** The "Great Recession." The housing market for exclusive lakefront property collapses.
- The Coastal Provinces have finished their mega-docks. They are now the largest manufacturer of inflatable pool toys.
- **2020:** The "Great Confinement." A mysterious "Pond Scum" outbreak forces everyone to stay in their own cottages and yell at each other via megaphone. It's the most peaceful period in decades.
- **2025:** The "Great Evaporation." The water level of Lake Meridian is noticeably dropping. The shorelines are expanding, creating new disputes over the newly exposed mudflats. A "Hair-Trigger" moment occurs when a Lower Shorian and an Upper Shorian fisherman get into a shoving match over a prime sunbathing spot.

### Main Characters (The "Diplomats")

- **Franklin "Franky" Delano (Upper Shoria):** A charming, wheelchair-using, chain-smoking political genius who guides his nation through the Great Depression and the Great Lakes War. He is known for his "Fireside Chats," which are just him talking really loudly from his porch.
- **Winston "Winnie" Churchill (Albion's Reach):** A grumpy, bulldog-loving, cigar-chomping orator. He spends most of his time on the lake in a tiny rowboat, shouting inspirational quotes at passing ducks.
- **Joseph "Uncle Joe" Vissarionovich (Lower Shoria):** A paranoid, mustachioed man who is convinced everyone is trying to steal his best fishing spot. He trusts no one, not even his own reflection.
- **Ronald "The Gipper" Reaganski (Upper Shoria, 1980s):** A former B-movie actor turned politician who famously told his Lower Shorian counterpart to "Tear down this ugly floating fence!" while on a scenic boat tour.
- **Deng "The Dock Builder" Xiaoping (Coastal Provinces):** A pragmatic leader who famously said, "It doesn't matter if the rowboat is red or blue, as long as it catches fish."
- **Boris "The Yeltsin" Nikolaevich (Lower Shoria, 1990s):** A chaotic leader who is frequently seen, in historical photos, trying to conduct a diplomatic summit while standing on a wobbly dock, holding a very large bottle of vodka.

### The Novel's Structure & Tone

The novel will be structured in five parts, mirroring the acts above. Each chapter will be a vignette, a "slice of lake life," that comically depicts a major historical event.

- **Tone:** Kurt Vonnegut meets *The Daily Show*. It's dry, satirical, and profoundly human. The humor comes from the immense gravity the characters give to their tiny, absurd problems.
- **Themes:** The pettiness of power, the absurdity of nationalism, the cyclical nature of history, and the universal desire for a nice, quiet spot by the water.

**Ending:** The novel ends in 2025, on the precipice of the "Great Evaporation." The nations are squabbling as usual, but for the first time, they all notice the water level is lower. They all stare out at the receding shoreline, a moment of shared, existential dread. Then, someone yells at someone else for having a better view. The argument resumes. The world, and the lake, go on.
