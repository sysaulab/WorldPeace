## 4. Long Amazon / Retail Description

**A novel of ideas, hope, and the long, slow work of building a world that can survive.**

In 1947, a linguist named Miriam Vance was recruited to a secret government project in the New Mexico desert. There, she heard a warning from the stars: humanity had one hundred years to restore the planet’s biodiversity—or be quarantined, contained, and managed forever.

The warning was buried. The lie was beautiful. The deadline was forgotten.

In 2030, a failed politician named Maya Chen lost an election and refused to give up. She had a plan to rebuild her neighbourhood from the ground up—not with charity, but with trust. Not with control, but with consent. Not with a government, but with a network. She called it the Last Mile.

In 2100, a homeless prophet found a booklet on a bench. It taught him three things: keep the feedback loop short. Ask genuine questions. Protect the fragile. He carried it into the dampened world, and the second track was reborn.

Across five centuries, from a secret desert base to a global web of peer-to-peer communities, *The Living Republic* follows the people who kept the truth alive—and the systems they built to help humanity govern itself without masters.

**For readers of Kim Stanley Robinson, Ursula K. Le Guin, and Becky Chambers, *The Living Republic* is a chronicle of the second track—a five-century epic of trust, cooperation, and the stubborn persistence of hope.**

**Praise for the idea:**
*“The truth is the only thing they can’t take. The stool is the only thing we can build together.”*

**What you’ll find inside:**
- A secret alien warning buried for a century

- A governance protocol based on continuous consent
- A three-part grammar of peace: the short loop, the open question, the fragile shield
- Intergenerational resistance against cognitive control
- A hopeful, slow-burn vision of a world after the cage
