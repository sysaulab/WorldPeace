
## 1. Space Segment: The Distributed Retina

**Hardware:**
- **CubeSat constellation (12U–16U form factor)**  
  Each satellite carries:
  - *Multispectral imager*: visible, near-infrared, shortwave infrared, and thermal bands. Enough to see through camouflage, smoke, and light cloud cover, and to detect heat signatures of vehicle engines or missile launches.
  - *Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR)* on later, more advanced units: all-weather, day/night imaging that can detect metal objects, track changes in ground disturbance, and count vehicles in a convoy.
  - *Software-Defined Radio (SDR)*: listens across a wide spectrum for radar emissions, military communication bands, and inadvertent RF leakage from weapon systems. Capable of triangulating emitters when multiple satellites pass overhead.
- **Inter-satellite links (laser or RF mesh)**  
  Cubesats form a low-earth-orbit mesh, relaying data in near real-time to ground stations without needing direct line-of-sight to a specific downlink site. This avoids dependence on a single nation’s ground infrastructure.
- **Onboard edge-AI processor** (e.g., Google Coral TPU or radiation-hardened FPGA): runs lightweight object-detection and change-detection models to flag anomalies (convoys, missile launchers, sudden heat blooms) before downlinking. This conserves bandwidth and allows the satellite to “think” a little before sending pixels.

**Openness:**
- All hardware designs, firmware, and AI models are fully open-source.  
- Launches are booked on commercial rideshare missions (SpaceX, ISRO, Ariane) with no national security riders—paid for by the Commons Eye Foundation, a non-profit funded by donations and small grants.

## 2. Ground Segment & Community Feeds: The Distributed Nerve Endings

The satellites are only one input. The real power is the **multi-source fusion**:

- **Ground stations:**  
  Low-cost, open-source receiving kits (Raspberry Pi + SDR dongle + simple Yagi antenna) are distributed to volunteers worldwide. They download raw satellite telemetry and imagery, then re-publish it to the decentralized network. A station costs under $200 and can be run by a school, a library, or an individual.

- **Open-source mobile app (Commons Eye Capture):**  
  Allows anyone, anywhere, to upload geotagged, timestamped media (photos, videos, audio) directly to the network. The app:
  - Signs the data with the user’s ephemeral identity key.
  - Collects sensor metadata (GPS, barometer, compass) to aid later verification.
  - Strips EXIF data that could endanger the uploader, unless they opt in to full attribution.
  - Chunks and submits the data to the decentralized store (IPFS).

- **Automated scrapers and APIs:**  
  - AIS (ship transponders), ADS-B (aircraft), public seismometers, weather radar, commercial satellite feeds with permissive licenses.
  - Citizen-run sensor networks: radiation monitors, air-quality sniffers, acoustic sensors near military bases.

## 3. Data Fabric: The Iroh Protocol (Real Tech, Fictional Name?)

The narrative mentions *iroh*—and in our real world, **Iroh** is an actual Rust library implementing the **InterPlanetary File System (IPFS)** and **IPLD** for content-addressed, peer-to-peer data. The Commons Eye would use exactly this stack:

- **Content addressing (CIDs):**  
  Every piece of data—a satellite image, a whistleblower document, a ship movement log—is hashed (SHA-256) and referenced by its cryptographic fingerprint. This makes the data immutable and tamper-evident. If a government alters a file, the CID changes, breaking the chain of trust.

- **Decentralized storage (IPFS + Filecoin/IPNI):**  
  Data is chunked and distributed across thousands of nodes run by volunteers, universities, and nonprofits. There is no central server to raid. The network naturally replicates popular or critical data; pinning services ensure long-term archival.

- **Pub/Sub messaging (libp2p gossipsub):**  
  Used for real-time alerts: new missile silo image? The network broadcasts a compact notification containing the CID and a schema tag, so that monitoring nodes can instantly fetch and analyze the new data.

- **Data lineage and trust (IPLD schemas + DAGs):**  
  Every observation is linked to its source, processing steps, and related observations through a Merkle DAG. If a ground station processes a raw satellite feed into an annotated map, that derivation is recorded. Anyone can trace back to the raw bits and verify independently.

This **Iroh-based fabric** means the network is physically decentralized, logically unified, and cryptographically self-verifying. No country can shut it down; the data lives wherever the community cares to keep it alive.

## 4. Intelligence Layer: AI & Human Curation

Raw data is noise. The Commons Eye transforms it into usable *intelligence* through an open, layered process:

- **Automated first-pass analysis:**
  - Object detection models (YOLOvX, DETR) trained on open-source military vehicle datasets (tanks, artillery, radars, missile launchers) run on satellite imagery.
  - Signal classifiers identify radar types (e.g., SA-20 vs SA-10) from SDR captures.
  - Change detection algorithms compare new images to historical baselines, highlighting new excavations, convoys, or missile transporter-erector-launcher (TEL) movements.

- **Decentralized verification & reputation:**
  - A reputation system (e.g., EigenTrust, or a simple web-of-trust on top of DIDs) allows analysts, journalists, and volunteers to stake their credibility on an assessment.
  - Suspicious claims are flagged and subjected to community review. Multiple independent nodes can run the same AI models on the same raw data to confirm results. If you claim there’s a tank column, you’d better cite the raw images and model version used—because others will check.

- **Human fusion cells:**
  - Distributed teams of OSINT enthusiasts (think Bellingcat on steroids) compile situational reports from multiple data streams. They annotate, cross-reference, and produce daily briefs that are themselves published back into the network as signed, versioned documents.

- **Privacy-preserving whistleblower system:**
  - Uses the Nym mixnet or Tor for anonymous upload, combined with zk-SNARKs to prove possession of a valid government credential without revealing identity. This is how “Node 0xFF” can prove they are a genuine insider without ever being traceable.

## 5. Public Access & Visualization

All of this is useless if no one can see it. The Commons Eye provides:

- **A public web dashboard (decentralized via IPFS hosting + DNS):**  
  Shows a live map of the world with satellite passes, observed military activity hotspots, nuclear silo status indicators (hatch open/closed), and crowd-sourced reports. Clicking a dot drills down to raw imagery, chain of custody, and community analysis.
- **REST APIs and streaming endpoints:**  
  So that newsrooms, civil society, and even governments can integrate the feed into their own systems. No API key required—just pull from the nearest IPFS gateway or pinning service.
- **Alerting system:**  
  Users can subscribe to real-time alerts for specific regions or threat types (e.g., “Nuclear silo hatch change within 500km of me”). Alerts are cryptographically signed by the publishing nodes, guaranteeing authenticity.

## 6. Governance & Sustainability

The network is a commons, so it needs commons governance:

- **The Commons Eye Protocol (CEP):**  
  A lightweight standard for data formats, metadata schemas, and verification processes, evolved openly like the IETF RFCs. Upgrades are proposed, discussed, and adopted by rough consensus of the community of node operators.
- **Funding:**  
  A decentralized autonomous organization (DAO) controls a treasury from donations and grants. Node operators can earn reputation tokens for contributing storage, bandwidth, or verified analysis, redeemable for nothing—it’s reputation, not profit. The ethos is gift economy: you contribute because a seeing world is safer than a blind one.
- **Ethical red lines:**  
  By communal agreement, the network does not track individual persons (no facial recognition), does not store unencrypted private communications, and does not sell data. The goal is **planetary transparency for systems of violence**, not universal surveillance.

## 7. The Unsevered Limb in Practice

When a military convoy moves at night, the flow looks like this:

1. Thermal satellite pass detects heat bloom → edge AI flags “possible convoy, 20+ vehicles.”  
2. Raw image + metadata is chunked, hashed, and injected into IPFS via the Iroh protocol.  
3. Pub/sub notification pings all monitoring nodes: “New observation, region X, threat level medium.”  
4. Ground station in a neighboring country downloads the CID, runs a more powerful object-detection model, confirms 23 vehicles, including 4 TELs.  
5. Verified analysis is signed by the ground station’s DID and published back to IPFS with a link to the raw image.  
6. A human fusion cell in Nairobi cross-references with AIS data showing an unusual Russian cargo ship nearby, producing a situational report.  
7. A journalist in Berlin receives an alert, pulls the verified report and raw data, and publishes a story with cryptographic proof.  
8. The world sees, the convoy pauses, diplomacy kicks in, and the body avoids another self-inflicted wound.

This is the technical face of #NOMORESECRETS: not magic, just a thousand cheap satellites, a peer-to-peer data fabric, open AI, and millions of people refusing to be kept in the dark about the knives pointed at their own flesh.
