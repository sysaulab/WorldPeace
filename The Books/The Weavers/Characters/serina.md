
## Logline
A woman living in a city park with her dog and cat turns the nightly ritual of building a safe, clean camp into an act of dignity and defiance against the systems that sweep her away—until one morning the rangers arrive with an ultimatum that threatens to separate her from the only family she has left.

---

## Character Profile: Serina

**Core identity:** A resilient, fiercely independent woman experiencing homelessness, who imposes order on chaos through nightly ritual.

**Current situation:**  
- Lives in a city park after her SUV was towed.  
- Shares her life with a dog and a cat.  
- Sets up her tent with meticulous care every evening.  
- Rakes the ground to remove illicit material—needles, trash, broken glass—before she will allow herself or her animals to settle.  
- Is enraged every morning when the “homeless rangers” arrive to clear the area.

**Key traits:**  
- Meticulous, orderly, exacting.  
- Deeply bonded to her animals.  
- Proud and private.  
- Distrustful of authorities and outsiders.  
- Capable of tenderness and immense endurance.  
- Quick to anger when her space or safety is violated.

**Core wound:**  
She has lost control over her housing, her privacy, and her sense of being seen as a person. The raking and tent ritual is her way of reclaiming that control.

**External desire:**  
A stable, secure environment where she and her animals can live without fear of being dispersed.

**Internal need:**  
To accept that stability may come from connection—not just from perfect order or self-reliance.

**Strength:**  
She creates home out of nothing. She protects her animals and maintains her standards even in impossible conditions.

**Flaw:**  
Her rigidity and distrust can push away people who might actually help her.

## Supporting Cast

**The Narrator / Observer**  
- Met Serina when she was still living in her car.  
- May be a neighbor, outreach volunteer, friend, or simply someone who notices her.  
- Serves as the reader’s entry point into Serina’s world.  
- Struggles with how to help without violating Serina’s autonomy.

**The Dog**  
- Loyal, protective, possibly the reason she refuses shelters that do not allow pets.  
- Represents unconditional love and the stakes of losing her chosen family.

**The Cat**  
- Independent, watchful, often hidden.  
- A symbol of Serina’s softer, more private side.

**The Homeless Rangers**  
- The cute name Serina and the narrator use for the city workers or park enforcement who sweep homeless encampments.  
- Mostly deal with homeless people, as far as Serina knows.  
- Not necessarily villains; they are the face of an indifferent system.  
- At least one may be conflicted or secretly sympathetic.

**Other Park Residents**  
- Allies, rivals, or background characters with their own rituals and survival strategies.  
- Could include a veteran, a young runaway, an elderly person, or someone struggling with addiction.

**Outreach Worker / Social Worker**  
- Tries to connect Serina to services.  
- Faces the same barriers: pet restrictions, waitlists, lack of trust.  
- May become an unexpected ally.

## Setting

**Primary location:**  
A city park, especially at dawn and dusk. The tent becomes a fragile home; the raked ground becomes a boundary between safety and danger.

**Tone of place:**  
Urban, exposed, noisy, but also capable of quiet beauty. The park is both refuge and battleground.

**Timeframe:**  
Contemporary, over a few weeks or months, with flashbacks to Serina’s life in her SUV and the day it was towed.

---

## Central Conflict & Stakes

Serina needs safety, stability, and the right to live with her animals. The city’s policies make her nightly home illegal. She must navigate a system that sees her as a problem to be moved rather than a person to be housed.

**External stakes:**  
Losing her tent, her spot, or her dog and cat.  
Being separated from the only beings that keep her grounded.

**Internal stakes:**  
Losing her sense of identity and dignity.  
Becoming invisible or broken by the constant erasure of her home.

---

## Themes

- Home as ritual and repetition, not just four walls.  
- Dignity under duress.  
- The invisible lives of unhoused women.  
- Animal companionship as family and survival.  
- The violence of bureaucracy.  
- Control versus chaos.  
- What it means to be “stable” when the world refuses you stability.

## Plot Structure Suggestions

### Act One: The Ritual
- Introduce Serina through the narrator’s eyes.  
- Show her meticulous tent setup and the raking of illicit material.  
- Establish her bond with the dog and cat.  
- Reveal the morning conflict with the homeless rangers.  
- Flashback: the SUV being towed, the loss of her last private space.  
- Inciting incident: a ranger issues a final warning or posts a sweeping notice; Serina’s camp will be cleared, and her animals may be taken if she cannot relocate.

### Act Two: The Search for Stable Ground
- Serina tries alternatives: shelters reject her because of her pets; housing lists are years long.  
- The narrator attempts to help, but Serina resists.  
- Tensions rise with other park residents.  
- A crisis: the dog becomes ill, the cat escapes, or the tent is damaged.  
- Serina’s routine begins to break down, revealing the fragility of her control.  
- A sympathetic ranger or outreach worker offers a possible path—but it requires trust Serina cannot easily give.

### Act Three: The Clearing
- Climax: the morning sweep arrives. Serina must decide whether to fight, flee, or accept help.  
- She defends her space, her animals, and her dignity.  
- The confrontation forces a choice: hold onto total self-reliance or allow someone in.  
- Resolution: not a fairy-tale ending, but a small, hard-won stability. Maybe she gets into a pet-friendly transitional program, or a legal safe-camping area, or a job that leads to a room. The animals stay with her. She is still raking, but now she is not invisible.

## Motifs & Scene Ideas

- **The rake:** A symbol of agency, order, and the refusal to live in filth.  
- **The tent:** A fragile boundary between inside and outside, safety and exposure.  
- **The morning sweep:** Recurring antagonist, a clock that resets her world every day.  
- **The illicit material:** The park’s dangers that Serina must clean away, contrasting with society’s view of her as the problem.  
- **The dog and cat:** Their reactions to the tent, the rangers, and strangers reveal Serina’s emotional state.  
- **Flashbacks to the SUV:** Show how she lost her car and how that loss still haunts her.  
- **A moment of kindness:** A stranger leaves food, or a ranger looks away, showing small cracks in the system.

---

## Narrative Voice Options

1. **First-person narrator** — the person who met Serina and is now telling her story. Allows for intimate observation and moral questioning.  
2. **Close third-person** — alternating between Serina and the narrator, revealing her interior world and the observer’s helplessness.  
3. **Epistolary / journal** — the narrator keeps a journal about Serina’s life, creating distance and reflection.

---

## Possible Endings

- **Hopeful but honest:** Serina finds a pet-friendly room or safe parking program, but the system remains broken for others.  
- **Bittersweet:** She keeps her animals and her spot for now, but the threat of the sweep never disappears.  
- **Tragic:** The city takes the animals or destroys her camp, and Serina must rebuild from nothing—again.  
- **Transformative:** The narrator becomes more involved, and the act of bearing witness changes both of them.

---

## Sensitivity Notes

- Avoid romanticizing homelessness or turning Serina into a saint.  
- Show her anger, pride, and flaws alongside her resilience.  
- Do not use her trauma as entertainment; focus on her agency.  
- Research pet-friendly homelessness services and city camping policies if grounding the novel in realism.  
- Consider consulting people with lived experience of homelessness and animal companionship.
