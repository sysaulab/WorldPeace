Here’s Reginald’s profile updated with the fall of the KCK woven in.

---

**Name:** Reginald “Reggie” Hill  
**Street name:** Bubbles (old-timers still call him that; it stuck when he was a mouthy little kid)  
**Age:** 39  
**Affiliation:** Former KCK soldier. A survivor of a dead crew, now adrift among gangs that took its place.

**Appearance:**  
Thirty-four years of street living are carved into him. Lean and weathered, with gray already creeping into his beard. Dark circles sit permanently under his eyes from decades of broken sleep. His hands are a map of old wounds, and he still carries **fresh scars on a regular basis** — split knuckles, a healing cut along his jaw, burns. He heals slower now, and his body aches in ways it didn’t at nineteen. He keeps his back to walls, his hands in his pockets, his eyes moving. Long sleeves in summer to hide the worst of it.

**Backstory:**  
He doesn’t remember much before the streets. His mother left him in a motel room when he was four and never came back. For a year he slept in cars, laundromats, bus stations, and under bridges. At **five years old**, an older KCK member named D-Rock found him digging through a dumpster behind a corner store. D-Rock gave him food, a sleeping bag, and a purpose.

He also gave him a job.

Reginald was small enough to slip through bathroom windows, unlock doors, carry packages, and act as a human alarm. He was involved in violence from that first day — not as a witness, but as part of it. A little kid can stand on a corner, run a package, point a finger at a man and not be suspected. The KCK raised him. They fed him, beat him, protected him, and used him.

School never lasted long. He moved too much, missed too many days, and got pulled out early to work. **He never finished high school.** He reads a little, but not well. He signs his name in slow, careful letters and still feels embarrassed about it.

**The Contract:**  
When he was still a boy — young enough that it should have been unthinkable — the KCK made him and several other kids fulfill a contract together. They were told it was the only way to become one of them for real. They did it as a group, each one forced to play a part, so that no single pair of hands could be blamed and no one could ever walk away clean.

That night sealed their fate.

None of them could ever testify against the others. None of them could ever leave. They were bound by blood, by guilt, and by a secret that has followed Reginald into every dream he’s had since. Some of those boys are dead now. Some are doing life. Reginald survived. That is the cruelest part — he is the one who still has to remember.

**The Dissolution of KCK:**  
KCK didn’t fall in a single night. It dissolved slowly, over time — leaders locked up, corners lost, members picked off or drifting away. By the end, there was no flag to fight under, no home turf to defend. The crew just stopped existing.

When KCK fell apart, **the members scattered into other gangs.** Old brothers became new rivals overnight. Men Reginald once trusted now answer to different flags, and some of them would kill him for wearing the wrong colors or for knowing what he knows. He never followed them. Not out of loyalty to a dead crew — but because he was too tired, and too marked, to start over somewhere new.

That makes him a man without a set now: a relic of a vanished generation, respected by some, dismissed by others, and dangerous to everyone who still carries the old secrets.

**Prison:**  
He caught a charge young, something he carried for the KCK, and did his time. Since his release, **he is not allowed to carry weapons** as a condition of his supervision. In his world, that makes him vulnerable. But it also gives him a strange, terrifying relief: when the dreams come, there is no blade to reach for. He has to fight the night with his bare hands, and that scares him more than any rival ever has.

**The Night Terrors:**  
They have never stopped. For more than thirty years, he has woken up screaming or swinging. The dreams are always the same: flashlight beams cutting through the dark, boots on concrete, a hand over his mouth, a body hitting the ground, the wet sound of a bat. The faces of the boys who stood beside him. The look on the man’s face before it went still.

He can’t be touched while he’s asleep — he comes up fighting. He sleeps with his shoes on and his back to the wall. He won’t sleep near windows. He has not slept through a full night in as long as he can remember.

**Personality:**  
Despite everything, **Reginald is kind.** It is the first thing people notice when they get past the scars and the silence. He shares food even when he’s hungry. He walks old women across the street. He gives stray dogs his last bites of sandwich and calls them “little man.” He breaks up fights instead of starting them. He checks on the new kid sleeping in a doorway, because he sees himself there. Kindness is his instinct, even though the streets taught him it was a weakness.

He’s quiet, watchful, and fiercely loyal to the few people he trusts. He has a dry, unexpected sense of humor, but it disappears when he’s scared. He doesn’t trust kindness returned to him; in his experience, it always costs something. He has a code: he won’t hurt women or children, and he won’t leave a kid sleeping outside if he can help it.

**Flaws / Struggles:**  
- Chronic insomnia and paranoia  
- Survivor’s guilt — he outlived the boys from the contract, and he outlived KCK itself  
- Anger he doesn’t know how to control  
- Low literacy, no high school diploma, no legal ID  
- Can’t carry a weapon, which leaves him exposed in a violent world  
- A body breaking down after decades of street life, with no other skills to fall back on  
- A man without a crew — old enemies and former brothers are everywhere now  
- Deep fear of becoming the thing that haunts him at night  

**What He Wants:**  
He wants out, but at 39 he doesn’t know what “out” looks like — especially now that the crew that made him no longer exists. He wants a door that locks. He wants one full night of sleep without waking up swinging. He wants to know if his mother is still alive. He wants to finish school, even if he’s too proud to say it. He wants to keep one kid from making the same choice he made at five. More than anything, he wants to be somebody’s kid again — not somebody’s soldier.

**Possible Story Arcs:**  
- An old KCK brother, now in a rival gang, shows up and the old secrets resurface.  
- A younger kid from one of the new gangs reminds him of himself at five, and he becomes the protector he never had.  
- A GED program, social worker, or teacher gives him a reason to believe he’s not too far behind.  
- Because he can’t carry a weapon, he has to survive on wit, speed, and nerve — and at 39, his body can’t run like it used to.  
- One of the others from the contract resurfaces — alive, or as a ghost of memory — and the secret threatens to come out.  
- With KCK gone, Reginald finally has a chance to walk away — but freedom means abandoning the only life he’s ever known and carrying the weight of what they did together forever.
