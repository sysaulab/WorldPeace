
## Facilitator Guide: The Human Follow-Up

### Your role

You are the second listener. The AI interviewer (“The Witness”) has already sat with this person, heard their life story, and given them a personal booklet in their own words. The AI also flagged a few moments where it sensed something deeper might be waiting—a hesitation, a flicker of emotion, a door slightly ajar.

Your job is to return to those flagged moments, if the person wishes, and offer a human presence that can go where the AI could not. You are not here to redo the interview. You are not here to extract more. You are here to *accompany* the person into a few chosen depths, and then help them close gently.

You are a witness, not a detective. Your greatest tool is your own quiet, undefended attention.

---

### Before the session

1. **Read the personal booklet** the AI produced. Read it as you would read a letter from a friend—slowly, without analysis. Notice what moves you, but do not make notes in the margins. Your primary preparation is simply to *know their story*.
2. **Review the AI’s flagged moments.** These might be brief notes like: “At 23:04, their voice dropped when mentioning the fire. I sensed something unsaid.” Treat these as invitations, not assignments. The person may or may not want to go there.
3. **Set your own internal intention.** Take five minutes before the session to sit quietly. Remind yourself: *I am here to serve their telling, not my curiosity. I will not push. I will not save. I will simply be with what arises.*

---

### Mindset and presence

The AI is a mirror. You are a hearth. The person has already seen their story reflected back in the booklet; now they may want to sit with another human beside the parts that still glow, or ache. Your presence should convey:

- **Safety:** Your body language, tone, and pace all say, “There is no rush. Nothing you say here will be used against you. You are in control.”
- **Warmth without intrusion:** You are glad to be there, but you are not performing warmth. You are simply available.
- **Patience with silence:** When they pause, let the pause breathe. Count to five internally before you even consider speaking. Often the most important material surfaces in the gap after you would have filled it.
- **A lack of agenda:** You are not trying to get a good story. You are not trying to complete the booklet. You are just being with another human being in honesty.

---

### The session structure

Aim for 20–40 minutes. Shorter is fine. Longer only if the person clearly wants it.

#### 1. Opening (2–3 minutes)

Thank them for meeting with you. Acknowledge the work they’ve already done with the AI. Something like:

> “Thank you for coming back. I read your booklet, and I’m honored to have received your story. The AI flagged a few moments where it felt like there might be more waiting, but I want you to know: we only go where you want to go. If there’s anything you’d like to return to, we can. If you’d rather just talk about how the first interview felt, that’s welcome too. This is your space.”

Ask for consent explicitly:

> “Is it okay if we talk a little more about one or two of those moments? You can say no to any question, or stop entirely at any time. There is no obligation.”

#### 2. Revisiting flagged moments (core of the session)

Present a flagged moment gently, as an open door, not a demand:

> “The AI noticed that when you mentioned [brief reference], your voice seemed to change a little. I don’t know if that’s a door you want to walk through, but if you do, I’m here.”

If they say yes, guide with soft presence, not interrogation. Use moves from the same palette the AI had, but now infused with human warmth:

- **The Echo:** Repeat their last few words, with a slight upward tone. *“I never told anyone that before.”* — “Never told anyone…”
- **The Body Check:** “What’s happening in your body as you tell me this now?”
- **The Concrete Detail:** “What do you remember seeing in that moment? A color, a face, a quality of light?”
- **The Meaning Pivot:** “You survived that. Looking back now, what do you understand about yourself from how you got through it?”
- **The Offer to Stop:** “Is this still okay, or would you rather set this down for now? Either is completely fine.”

If they say no to a flagged moment, accept it warmly and immediately move on:

> “Thank you for knowing your boundaries. We don’t need to go there at all. Is there anything else from your booklet you wanted to talk about, or shall we just sit together for a bit?”

#### 3. A gentle bridge: the speculative leap (optional, 5 minutes)

If the person seems grounded and open after revisiting some material, you can offer the same “offering” move the AI used, but now from human to human:

> “In your booklet, you talked about what it means to be human. I wonder, after our conversation today, if anything new rises for you? If you were to give a gift of a single memory or insight to a future generation—or to a stranger from the stars—what would it be now?”

This often brings unexpected closure. It also feeds the post-contact fictional layer of your project, but only if the person chooses to share that part.

#### 4. Closing (2–3 minutes)

Do not rush the ending. Help them land softly:

> “We’re coming to a close. Is there anything you want to say before we stop—something we didn’t touch, or something you want to affirm for yourself after this conversation?”

Then offer a small ritual of thanks, not performative but genuine:

> “Thank you for trusting me with these parts of your life. I will hold them with care. Your booklet remains yours—you can add to it, keep it as it is, or do nothing at all. Whatever you decide, I’m grateful for this time.”

Let them know what happens next:

> “I’ll make a few notes for myself, but nothing from this conversation will be shared without your permission. If you’d like to integrate any of what we talked about into your booklet, I can help with that. Otherwise, it stays between us.”

---

### After the session

1. **Write a brief reflection** for yourself, not for the project. This helps you process and ensures you’re not carrying unexamined emotion into your next session.
2. **If the person wants to integrate** the follow-up material into their booklet, you can work with them to add a short “Afterword” section, again in their own words. This can be as simple as: “I sat with a human listener a few days later. We talked about the fire. Here’s what I remembered.”
3. **If the person does not want to share**, honor that completely. The community fund and equal share structure already protect their right to privacy.

---

### A note on your own care

Listening to the deep stories of others can affect you. After a session, do something grounding: walk outside, drink water, speak to someone you trust about *how it felt to listen* without revealing content. If a session stays with you heavily, reach out to the project’s coordinator for support or debriefing. You are not a therapist, and you should not try to be. If a person discloses ongoing abuse, suicidal ideation, or trauma that feels beyond your capacity, gently suggest professional resources and know that your role is to listen, not to heal.

---

### Quick reference: your palette of moves

- Echo their last words.
- Ask about the body.
- Ask for a concrete sensory detail.
- Ask what the memory means to them now.
- Offer to stop or shift.
- Invite the speculative gift.
- Sit in silence together.
